Academic · Teaching

Teaching

Graduate and undergraduate courses, open course materials, and a teaching practice built around access, openness, and making real things.

← Back to Academic

01 How I teach

Twenty years of college teaching: composition and reading at Humboldt State University and College of the Redwoods (2004–2009), then fourteen years at Western Oregon University teaching graduate educational technology and learning design, plus undergraduate honors, first-year, and writing courses — most of it online, much of it asynchronous. The through-line is the same question that runs under the scholarship: who gets access to knowledge, media, tools, and participation?

  • Access first. Every course is built captioned, transcribed, and keyboard-navigable before it is built pretty. Accessibility is a design constraint, not a retrofit — and students learn to work the same way.
  • Open by default. Course maps, syllabi, and readings are openly licensed and indexed at Open Oregon wherever possible, so the materials outlive the term and the paywall.
  • Adults, not pupils. Graduate students arrive with careers and expertise. Courses draw on that experience, let students choose problems that matter to their own classrooms and workplaces, and treat the instructor as a designer of conditions rather than a dispenser of content. (More on this under Andragogy.)
  • Make real things. Portfolios, professional projects, OERs, videos, and conference proposals — artifacts students can actually use after the grade is posted.
  • Theory with a job to do. Communication theory, user experience, usability, and critical perspectives on technology (including AI) show up as working tools for evaluating what gets adopted in schools, not as decoration.

02 Graduate courses

MSEd coursework at Western Oregon University, 2012–present. Most are three-credit online courses; the 2026 program revision moved several to four credits.

  • CSE 604 — Portfolio. Capstone portfolio for degree completers; redesigned in 2023 to be practical for finishing students and to end the Incomplete/Re-register pattern.
  • CSE 616 — Designing & Teaching Online Courses. Accessible, usable online course design; openly licensed course documents.
  • CSE 619 — Big Thinkers in Educational Technology. A syllabus of free and open readings on the field’s foundational and critical voices.
  • CSE 624 — Open Educational Resources (earlier: Internet for Educators). Finding, critiquing, and building OERs; revised 2021–2022.
  • CSE 626 — Instructional Design. Originally structured around ADDIE (2012–2013); revised 2021 to reflect current ID practice and theory.
  • CSE 627 — Social Media & Web Tools for Teaching and Learning. Redesigned in the Spring 2023 OER Sprint; openly licensed course map.
  • CSE 629 — Web-Based Website Design.
  • CSE 632 — Cultural, Social, and Philosophical Issues in Educational Technology.
  • CSE 640 — Social Media & Identity (developed 2025).
  • CSE 654 — Graduate Professional Practice (1 credit; first offered Fall 2026). A three-course series on research, writing, and conference presenting for graduate students.
  • CSE 680 — Communication Theories. Revised 2020 to connect theory to user experience and usability.
  • CSE 686 — AI, Emerging Technologies, Teaching, Training, and Learning. Revised 2021, 2022, and 2023 — from surveillance/AR/VR toward AI’s impact on teaching and critiques of AI-driven tools.
  • ED 610 — Adult Learning Theory and Practice. Openly licensed course documents.
  • ED 633 — Research and Writing. Research methods and literature review for graduate students.
  • ED 640 — Professional Writing in Education (4 credits; first offered Spring 2027). Translating scholarly research into practitioner- and public-facing writing.
  • CSE 508 workshops — Accessibility (2022): applying accessibility principles to images, audio, video, and courses. Captivate (2021): Adobe Captivate and Articulate Rise.
  • Earlier courses (2012–2016) — CSE 606 Web-Based Web Site Design; CSE 617 Open Source Tools; CSE 667 Making Video Accessible; CSE 668 Creating Mobile Content; CSE 669 Screencasting; CSE 692 Learning Objects.

03 Undergraduate, honors & writing courses

Since 2022, a second strand of face-to-face and hybrid undergraduate teaching — mostly new courses built from scratch.

  • WR 401 — Writing Across Media (4 credits; 2025). Writing, media, AI, and accessibility; openly licensed course map.
  • WR 301 — Integrating Writing & Design (4 credits; 2025).
  • WR 400 — Diversity & Inclusion in Professional Writing.
  • FYS 107 — Visuals for Everyone: Inclusive Media Design (4 credits; 2025).
  • FYS 107 — Taylor-Made Rhetoric: A Multimedia Journey through Swift’s Work (4 credits; 2024). Openly licensed course map.
  • HNR 301 — Queen’s Gambit: Chess in Mass Media & Culture (4 credits; 2025).
  • HNR 301 — Digital Inclusivity: A Deep Dive into Accessible Media Creation (3 credits; 2024).
  • CSE 486 — Emerging Technologies in Teaching and Learning (2024). New course, with CSE 408, a paired one-credit workshop on presenting emerging tools and AI.
  • CSE 469 — Hey U Up? (4 credits; 2022, 2024). Communication, technology, and relationships through critical and usability lenses.
  • ED 270 — Technology for Teachers.

Before WOU (2004–2009): first-year and developmental reading and writing at Humboldt State University and College of the Redwoods, including a composition course developed for nursing students.

05 Graduate supervision & program work

  • Capstones. Supervised more than seventy-five master’s capstones — Learning Design & Technology portfolios, professional projects, theses, and comprehensive exams — from inception to completion since 2021, and scores more since 2012, across the EdTech, Literacy, ESOL, LDT, and Interdisciplinary programs.
  • Individualized study. Nineteen supervised independent-study courses and twenty by-arrangement sections for forty-two students (2021–2025), so students who need a course can take it when the rotation doesn’t offer it.
  • Program coordination. MSEd Program Coordinator since June 2024; Educational Technology & Instructional Design certificate coordinator since 2020. Led the 2023–2024 revision into the MSEd in Learning Design & Technology and co-led the 2026 merger of three MSEds into the MSEd in Curriculum & Instruction Design — 48 course changes, nine programs and certificates revised, closed, or combined.
  • Mentoring. Supervised a graduate assistant in social-media marketing, AI content creation, and accessible content (2023–2024); Faculty Mentor, Live/Learn program (2013–2016); HASTAC Scholar mentor (2012–2013).

06 Faculty development & workshops

  • Co-founded and coordinated ASSETT (Agile and Sustainable Supplemental Educational Technology Training), 2012–2016: workshops on captioning your own and others’ video, accessible syllabi (including for D/deaf faculty), screencasting, SWIVL, classroom hardware, and a Teacher Technology Training Fair.
  • Coordinated campus visits by Punya Mishra, Jesse Stommel, and Audrey Watters (2014–2016).
  • Accessible Online Teaching: Fundamentals — WOU Summer Online Teaching Institute (2018); invited captioning session for INT 523, Interpreters and Interpreter Educators (2018).
  • Visiting faculty, Tianhua University, Shanghai (September 2017 and 2018).
  • E-Learning Consultant, National Center on Deaf-Blindness (2012–2015); Accessibility Consultant, CCCC 2018.
