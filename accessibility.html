Access & Openness

Accessibility Statement

What this site aims for, how it’s tested, what’s not done yet, and how to tell me when something doesn’t work.

Our commitment

Accessibility is the subject of this site and its standard. I am working to make every page usable by as many people as possible, including people who use screen readers, keyboard-only navigation, magnification, or reduced-motion settings.

Scope

This statement covers the first-party pages at zobelg.github.io (home, academic and teaching pages, access & openness, art & AAA, writing, experiments, video, about, and these statement pages). It does not cover third-party sites linked from here (for example Substack, Gumroad, or the Netlify experiments), which have their own accessibility practices.

Target

The goal is WCAG 2.2 Level AA (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines), which also covers the 2.1 requirements. The site is currently working toward that target, not yet formally conformant — see known limitations below.

What’s in place

  • Semantic headings, landmarks, and lists; one clear page title and h1 per page.
  • A skip-to-content link and visible keyboard focus on every page.
  • Colour combinations checked for contrast; links underlined, not signalled by colour alone.
  • Reduced-motion support (animation and smooth scrolling are disabled when requested); no autoplay.
  • Navigation targets at least 44×44 pixels; responsive layouts that reflow on small screens.
  • The videos are silent and each has a text version of its on-screen text.
  • The body typeface is Atkinson Hyperlegible, designed for low-vision readability.

Known limitations

  • The site is still a working draft; some pages are placeholders and are marked as such.
  • Full manual testing with screen readers (VoiceOver, NVDA), keyboard-only navigation, 200–400% zoom, custom text spacing, and forced-colors mode is still in progress.
  • Some text inside video frames uses red on black; small text in that combination may not meet AA contrast. Each clip’s text version provides an accessible equivalent.
  • Fonts currently load from Google Fonts; self-hosting is planned.

How it’s tested

To date: automated HTML validation, contrast calculations, link checking, and visual review on desktop and narrow screens. Planned: the manual assistive-technology testing listed above.

Report a problem

If you hit a barrier on this site, please tell me and I’ll fix it. Email zobelg@wou.edu with the page address and a short description of the problem and your setup (browser and any assistive technology). I aim to respond within a few business days.

Statement date: draft, June 2026. Updated as remediation and testing continue.
