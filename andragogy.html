Academic · Teaching

Andragogy

Andragogy — the theory and practice of how adults learn — and how it shapes my course design.

← Back to Academic

01 Who I teach

Most students I teach are working adults: teachers, trainers, librarians, instructional designers. People with years of working experience. Thus this page focuses on andragogy, adult learning, rather than pedagogy. The difference matters. Adults arrive with expertise, limited time, and real settings where course work will land.

Earlier in my career, I believed I could not offer a single, seamless teaching philosophy that describes how I teach — instead, I offer a web of commitments. A rhizome, to borrow Deleuze and Guattari’s term for roots without a trunk. That’s still true. Over the past seven years, with close review and intention, several themes — threads or roots — show in the courses. They are created to be usable, accessible, and transparent so learners know how courses were built and how courses are graded.

02 The five strongest roots in this rhizome

  • Guide, not gatekeeper. Students regularly choose the medium for their responses and bring their professional contexts to projects. If assignments do not connect to their real work, I want to know so that we can adapt and adjust.
  • Show the reasoning. Policies come with a why. Readings have framing as to why they matter. Grading uses plain scales. Where a choice is deliberate, I say so before students start.
  • Students are competent professionals, often better than I am with a tool. I assume competence, ask about their contexts, and design work whose results I cannot fully predict. When I have no examples, I say so. When the course contains an error, correcting it is my job, not theirs.
  • Accessibility by construction, not by request. Captioned videos, transcripts, alt text, and plain-language summaries are how my courses are envisioned and created; they are not extras added later. Students produce accessible media, too: correcting transcripts to WCAG 2.2, building captions, writing alt-text. This is where my teaching meets my research on captioning.
  • Honest about AI use, mine and theirs. I disclose how AI supports course design and what decisions stayed mine. Students disclose and verify their own use. No one is required to buy a premium tool. Unless a course is explicitly about AI, students can opt out of AI use with alternative assignments.
