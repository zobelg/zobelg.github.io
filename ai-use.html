AI Use Statement

How AI tools are — and aren’t — used in this work. A living document.

Nearly everything on this site was made with AI tools in the loop. This page says where, how, and what stays human — so the use is visible rather than hidden.

The short version

I work with AI collaboratively: I set the direction, supply the source material, and make the final call; AI tools draft, revise, generate, format, and check. Everything published under my name has been reviewed and revised by me. The arguments, the scholarship, the creative direction, and the editorial judgment are mine.

Tools

As of October 2026: Claude (Anthropic), GPT (OpenAI), and Perplexity for drafting, revising, organizing, coding, and research; ElevenLabs for voice, transcription, and audio; Leonardo for image generation. The mix changes; this list is updated when it does.

This website

The site’s HTML, CSS, and structure were scaffolded and iterated with AI assistance. Page copy was drafted with AI from my CV, course materials, and notes, then reviewed and revised by me. AI also helped organize content across pages, run HTML validation and contrast checks, and build the web version of my CV from the master document. The accessibility work is partly automated and partly not — see the accessibility statement for exactly which is which.

Academic and teaching materials

Course materials, syllabi, open educational resources, handouts, and course videos are drafted, formatted, and revised with AI assistance. Course videos may use an AI-narrated learning assistant alongside my own recordings. Captions and transcripts for my videos are generated with AI transcription and then corrected by hand; where accuracy matters, human review is the standard. Book reviews, articles, and conference talks are my own writing; AI is used for research, outlining, and revision, not for the claims or the judgments.

Art, zines, and détournements

The Association of Autonomous Access work is made collaboratively with AI on purpose — it is part of what the work is about. Zine text, layouts, and graphics are developed with AI and revised by me; the AAA zines 1–18 and the détournement series were co-selected, revised, and created with AI to explore the intersections of accessibility, art, automation, and AI. Panel art for the teaching comics, posters, and kinetic-text videos is AI-generated from my scripts and direction, then edited. The ideas, the politics, and the selection are human.

Caption fiction and writing

Caption fiction — stories told through closed-captioning conventions — is written collaboratively with AI: I set the premise, the form, and the rules; AI drafts; I revise and decide. Essays on the Substack are drafted and revised with AI assistance. Any piece presented as mine has been reviewed and revised by me.

What stays human

The arguments, scholarship, creative direction, and final editorial judgment are mine. Claims of fact are checked against sources. Nothing is published under my name that I have not reviewed and stand behind.

Access and labor

Much of my work concerns captioning, transcripts, and the labor behind them. I name that tension directly: automated tools can expand access, but they can also displace paid human captioners and reproduce error and bias. Where accuracy and credit matter — captions, transcripts, scholarship — human work is the standard, and AI does not replace it.

Transparency

If you have questions about how a specific piece was made, ask: zobelg@wou.edu.
